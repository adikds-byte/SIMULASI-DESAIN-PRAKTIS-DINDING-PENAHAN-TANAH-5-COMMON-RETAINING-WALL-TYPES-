A. Quick Comparison Summary — 5 Common Retaining Wall Types

Wall Type Best Use Main Resistance Typical Height Advantages Limitations
1. Gravity Wall Low retaining heights Self-weight of mass concrete / masonry 2 – 6 m • Simple design
• Durable		 • Heavy section
• Not economical for tall walls
2. Cantilever RC Wall Most general applications Cantilever action of stem & base + soil on heel 3 – 8 m • Most common
• Efficient		 • Base slab needs more excavation
3. Counterfort Wall High retaining walls Stem panels + counterforts 6 – 12 m • Economical for tall walls • More formwork
• Complex detailing
4. Anchored / Tieback Deep excavations, limited space Tieback anchors + embedment 4 – 15 m • Saves space
• Suitable for urban areas		 • Anchor installation
• Higher initial cost
5. MSE Wall (Reinforced Soil) Highways, embankments, bridges Reinforced soil mass (geogrid/strip) 3 – 20 m • Very economical
• Fast construction		 • Needs quality backfill & compaction

B. Key Learning Points

  • Gravity walls rely on self-weight to resist lateral active earth pressure.
  • Cantilever RC walls are the most practical and widely used for moderate heights (3–8 m).
  • Counterfort walls are economical for taller structures (> 6 m) by reducing stem bending.
  • Anchored / Tieback walls are ideal for deep urban excavations with restricted space.
  • MSE walls are modern, flexible, and construction-friendly for highways & embankments.
